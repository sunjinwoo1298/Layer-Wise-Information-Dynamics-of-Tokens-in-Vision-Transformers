# Research results interpretation — Phase 4 confirmatory benchmark (DeiT-Tiny, P4-CONFIRM, n = 2,985)

Written for: the research team preparing a paper (readers who know the project but need a cautious reading of the frozen confirmatory outputs). Every number below is taken from `confirmatory_analysis/` (see `publication_tables/`); mechanisms are labelled as hypotheses where no ablation tested them. Differences are method − comparator in percentage points (pp). Limitations and the data-access incident are in `limitations_and_disclosures.md` and must travel with any use of these results.

## 1. What the confirmatory results establish
Within the frozen design (training-free inference-time adaptations of `deit_tiny_patch16_224`, a balanced 995-class ImageNet-validation subset, configurations chosen on a disjoint development split, analytic FLOPs matched within ±1 pp):

1. **ToMe preserved the unpruned model's predictions and ground-truth accuracy better than the Phase 3 geometric prune-only schedule in all 16 primary cells**, at every budget (≈40/50/60/70 % FLOPs) and both stage patterns. Agreement differences ranged from +9.2 pp (B70) to +18.6 pp (B40); top-1 differences from +3.4 pp to +12.9 pp. All 32 comparisons were Holm-significant (Tables 2a/2b). The bootstrap CIs exclude zero in every cell.
2. **EViT-style (our reproduction-inspired, training-free variant) was budget-dependent against geometric**: significantly worse at ≈40 % FLOPs (agreement −8.6/−7.7 pp; top-1 −7.6/−6.8 pp), indistinguishable at ≈50 % (|diff| ≤ 0.54 pp agreement, ≤ 0.07 pp top-1; CIs span zero), and significantly better at ≈60 % and ≈70 % (agreement +3.3 to +4.0 pp; top-1 +1.7 to +2.2 pp) (Tables 3a/3b).
3. **The Phase 3 allocator did not show a broad advantage over geometric.** Averaged over the 24 replication comparisons the difference was +0.62 pp agreement (15 positive, 9 negative); only 1 of 24 was Holm-significant (B40, 2-8-10, floor 32: +2.48 pp), and 3 of 24 for top-1 (Table 4).
4. Where ToMe was compared with the other Phase 3 variants (proposed floor-16, single-stage, constrained floor-16, unconstrained equal-drop), it was better in every defined cell, all Holm-significant (Table 5). EViT-style was mixed against these (Section 3).

What they do **not** establish: superiority at other model sizes, datasets or hardware; superiority of ToMe over any method not in the benchmark; any statement about latency; or that the differences would persist under different (e.g. fine-tuned) conditions. P4-DEV results were used for selection and are not part of this evidence.

## 2. Why ToMe may outperform the geometric schedule (hypotheses, not tested here)
The data show *that* ToMe is better, not *why*. Plausible, untested explanations: (i) ToMe merges similar tokens and carries their information forward (size-weighted, with proportional attention), whereas the Phase 3 schedules permanently discard tokens at three layers; (ii) ToMe reduces tokens gradually from the first block, so no single layer removes a large fraction at once; (iii) the Phase 3 schedules were not designed for merging-style information retention. A controlled ablation (e.g. prune-only with ToMe's schedule, or merge with Phase 3's schedule) would be needed to separate these; none was run. Note also that FLOPs are matched only within ±1 pp, not exactly: ToMe's realized FLOPs were slightly lower than geometric at B40/B50/B60 and ≈0.5–0.6 pp higher at B70 (exact values in Tables 2a/2b and 7), so the B70 gap should be read with that offset in mind.

## 3. Why EViT-style results are budget-dependent
The loss at ≈40 % FLOPs is large (κ = 0.32 retained at each of three layers) while the gain at ≈60–70 % is modest. Candidate explanations (untested): the training-free setting means DeiT-Tiny was never adapted to token reorganization, so aggressive keep-rates compound errors; the fused token is a raw attention-weighted sum, a documented deviation from the paper's description; the schedule is fixed to layers 4/7/10, so budget is only controlled through κ. EViT-style is **not** official EViT (no training, no warm-up) and its numerical parity is unverified, so these results speak about this reproduction-inspired variant only. Against other Phase 3 comparators the picture is similar: versus proposed floor-16 it was significantly worse at B40 (−10.6/−9.6 pp agreement), not different at B50, and significantly better at B60/B70; versus single-stage it was worse at B40 (−3.65 pp agreement, −3.55 pp top-1, significant) and better at B50–B70 (Table 5).

## 4. Why the Phase 3 replication does not establish broad superiority
The 24 replication comparisons are 4 budgets × 2 patterns × 3 floors, but the frozen file contains only ten distinct schedule vectors, so the comparisons are not 24 independent schedules. One Holm-significant cell out of 24, with a mean difference under 1 pp and sign changing across cells, is consistent with no systematic advantage and with the Phase 3 confirmatory conclusion (no superiority over geometric). It is also a replication of the *method* on a new 995-class, 2,985-image set, not an exact replication of the Phase 3 evaluation. The single significant cell should be treated as a possible chance finding among 24 tests (it survives Holm correction at the stated family size, but was not a prespecified headline).

## 5. DiffRate: what the supplementary results suggest and why they stay supplementary
In the exploratory native-FLOPs track (DiffRate's released DeiT-Tiny rates 0.6–0.9 at their executed analytic FLOPs of 52.19/57.92/64.47/71.92 %), DiffRate had higher point estimates than 35 of 36 matched comparators for both metrics, 33 Holm-significant (Table 6). **Against ToMe at matched FLOPs** the picture is much closer (Table 6b): the difference was significant only at DR0.6 (agreement +2.28 pp, Holm-adj p ≈ 0.002; top-1 +1.81 pp, ≈ 0.003); at DR0.7–DR0.9 the differences (+1.04, +0.54, −0.50 pp agreement) were not significant. This suggests learned/searched merge-and-prune rates and ToMe are competitive at the higher budgets in this setting, and that the large advantage over Phase 3 prune-only schedules is a property of merging-style methods rather than of any one of them. These results remain supplementary because: the merge semantics of our DiffRate adaptation were not verified against official execution; target 1.0 is not applicable as released (excluded); the native FLOPs differ from the primary 40/50/60/70 grid; the comparators were regenerated per cell (ToMe/EViT-style tuned on P4-DEV, Phase 3 re-selected on a 500-image dev subset), so selection histories differ; and the track is exploratory with its own Holm family.

## 6. Prediction agreement versus ground-truth accuracy
Agreement is fidelity to the unpruned model's top-1 prediction; top-1 is correctness against the labels. They answer different questions and are never combined. The unpruned model has top-1 = 2,086/2,985 (69.89 %, derived from the saved reference array). A method can keep most of the model's predictions yet lose accuracy (ToMe B50: 83.0 % agreement but 65.3 % top-1), and agreement does not imply accuracy because the unpruned model is itself wrong on ~30 % of images; when a pruned model changes a wrong prediction to a right one, agreement falls while accuracy rises. Statements should therefore name the metric. In the primary results the two metrics agreed in direction and significance pattern for ToMe and (apart from small shifts in effect size) for EViT-style, but this was verified cell by cell, not assumed.

## 7. FLOPs and latency are not interchangeable
All FLOPs figures are analytic counts from realized token counts (patch embedding and head fixed), identical in convention across methods. They exclude auxiliary cost (matching/merging/scoring/fusion), which is reported separately (Table 7: analytic auxiliary FLOPs per image are larger for ToMe than for EViT-style, and measured auxiliary seconds in the confirmatory run were also larger; these were taken on a shared CPU session and are indicative only). Latency was measured **only on P4-DEV** (Gate 4), on CPU, with wide IQRs; in those measurements ToMe at B60/B70 ran *slower* than its own unpruned reference in the explicit-attention engine (ratios 1.04 and 1.20) despite 60–70 % of the FLOPs, and different engines (explicit-attention vs fused-attention) are not comparable in absolute milliseconds. Hence no latency ranking is claimed, and a method's FLOPs advantage must not be read as a speed advantage.

## 8. Language to use / avoid
Use: "in this benchmark, at matched analytic FLOPs (±1 pp), ToMe preserved prediction agreement and top-1 accuracy better than the Phase 3 geometric schedule in all primary cells"; "EViT-style results depended on the budget"; "the Phase 3 allocator showed no consistent advantage over geometric (1 of 24 Holm-significant)". Avoid: "ToMe is superior to all pruning methods", "the allocator is better/worse than geometric" without the replication caveats, any latency or wall-clock claim from FLOPs, and "P4-CONFIRM was never loaded/untouched".
